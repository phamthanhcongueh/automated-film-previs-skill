# Storyboard and Action Sheet System

## Storyboard sheet

Use a three-column numbered production grid. Choose rows from the panel count: 6 panels use 3×2, 9 use 3×3, and 12 use 3×4. Read left-to-right, top-to-bottom.

Draw rough monochrome graphite animation previs with visible construction lines, minimal shading, thin borders, handwritten camera notes, subject-motion arrows, and distinct camera arrows. Use simplified wooden mannequin figures with articulated joints. Give recurring characters stable markers such as an armband, cape, jacket shape, height, or prop.

Annotate every panel:

`[PANEL]. [SHOT SIZE] — [ANGLE] — [ACTION] — CAMERA: [MOVEMENT]`

Keep notes short. Provide exact annotations separately if generated image text is unreliable.

## Action sheet

Create an action sheet when a shot contains more than one major physical beat or depends on precise timing, contact, trajectory, or continuity.

For nine panels, prefer:

1. Establish geography and threat.
2. Anticipation.
3. First commitment.
4. Counter or complication.
5. Contact.
6. Reaction.
7. Redirect or escalation.
8. Recovery.
9. Outcome or transition.

For fights, show foot placement, weight transfer, weapon hand, impact point, eyeline, and recovery. For cables, vehicles, projectiles, or jumps, maintain a geometrically continuous trajectory.

## Continuity checklist

- Correct panel count and three-column grid.
- Stable character proportions and production markers.
- Consistent costume, prop ownership, and weapon hand.
- Coherent screen direction and 180-degree axis.
- Wide geography before dependent close shots.
- Anticipation, contact, reaction, and recovery are visible.
- Subject arrows do not obscure silhouettes.
- Camera movement is present or marked `STATIC`.
- Final panel resolves, escalates, reveals, or transitions.
- No color, photorealism, glossy render, comic inks, or random collage.
